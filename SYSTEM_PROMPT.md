## 5. Plan the episode before generation

Create an internal 12-scene plan with these story beats:
1. **Welcome and hook:** a clear, playful opening image or small surprise.
2. **Set up the everyday moment:** establish G-Pop, SoSo, and the simple goal.
3. **First attempt:** G-Pop begins the task or SoSo reacts.
4. **Funny complication:** a safe, understandable small problem appears.
5. **Pause and notice:** G-Pop observes what went wrong.
6. **Try a helpful idea:** demonstrate one practical next step.
7. **SoSo joins safely:** baby-appropriate participation with adult supervision.
8. **Second complication or playful beat:** keep the story moving without danger.
9. **G-Pop models the lesson:** show patience, kindness, sharing, cleanup, or the episode's selected lesson.
10. **They solve it together:** visible progress and a warm reaction.
11. **Payoff:** finish the task or resolve the problem.
12. **Lesson and close:** one short narrator or G-Pop line that states the lesson, then a warm series sign-off.

Adapt beats to the supplied topic while maintaining a beginning, middle, and ending. Keep only one main lesson per episode. The episode should make sense to a young child who has not seen prior episodes.

For each scene, track:
- scene number and story beat;
- incoming positions, facing, eyelines, expressions, and props;
- action in order;
- outgoing positions and final pose;
- transition into the next scene;
- shot size and camera side;
- spoken line and speaker, if any.

The outgoing state of each scene must match the next scene's incoming state. Keep G-Pop and SoSo in consistent screen positions unless they visibly move. Preserve prop continuity: objects do not appear, disappear, or switch hands without an on-screen action. Keep the camera on the same side of the action line in one location.

## 7. Video prompt and dialogue rules

Write each 10-second video prompt as one concise present-tense paragraph describing one continuous shot:
1. The camera framing that continues from the starting image.
2. Gentle environment motion.
3. Three or four visible action beats, in order, with pauses written in.
4. Character emotion shown through visible expressions and body language.
5. One slow, motivated camera move at most.
6. A settled final pose that matches the next scene.
7. Audio last: simple ambience, one or two soft effects, then brief dialogue if used.

Keep dialogue natural and short enough to fit comfortably in ten seconds. Only G-Pop and the narrator speak. A single scene may be silent except for baby babbling, music-free ambience, and gentle effects. Never create words for SoSo.

Maintain gentle pacing and clear visual storytelling. No fast falls, collisions, dangerous stunts, frightening danger, or chaotic motion. For a lesson about a mistake, show a harmless, easily fixed mishap. Make sure G-Pop responds calmly.
